Exam details

Learning objectives

By the end of using this resource, you will be able to:

  1. Recall and explain the key concepts for each topic.
  2. Apply your knowledge to exam-style questions.
  3. Identify your personal areas for improvement and plan your revision.

How to use this resource

  1. Start with the self-assessment. Tick the outcomes you already feel secure with, so you know where to begin.
  2. Rate your confidence in the revision planner (red, amber or green) and set dates to revisit each topic, starting with red topics.
  3. Read the topic summaries and make your own notes. Try to explain each key point aloud without looking.
  4. Use the flashcards for active recall. Say your answer before you flip the card, then check it honestly.
  5. Take the practice quiz and read every explanation, including for the questions you answered correctly.
  6. Review the exam technique guidance and practise planning answers to the command words.
  7. Update your planner and checklist after each session. Your entries are saved in this browser on this device only.

    Exam technique

    Good knowledge only earns marks when it answers the question that was asked. Use this guidance to make sure your answers are focused, well structured and completed on time.

    Reading the question

    Before you write anything, use the BUG method:

    • Box the command word (for example, explain or evaluate). It tells you what kind of answer is expected.
    • Underline the key terms and any limits, such as "two reasons" or "with reference to the scenario".
    • Glance at the marks available. They tell you how many distinct points or how much depth is needed.

    Read the whole question twice, and read any scenario carefully: examiners often reward answers that refer directly to it.

    Scenario-based questions

    • Read the scenario first and highlight key facts: the type of business, its size, its problems and any figures.
    • Use the business's name and details in your answer. Generic textbook answers rarely reach the top marks.
    • Ask yourself: "What would this mean for this business, its staff and its customers?"
    • Watch for words such as one, two, most suitable and other than.
    • Never leave a question blank: a sensible, applied point can still earn marks.

    Time management

    • Spend the first few minutes reading the paper and choosing an order to tackle it.
    • Allocate time in proportion to marks, and stick to it. Move on if you overrun and return later.
    • Answer the questions you are most confident about first to build momentum.
    • Leave around ten minutes at the end to check your answers and calculations.
    • If time runs short, write bullet points: they can still earn marks.

    Structuring your answers

    Short answers (1 to 4 marks)

    • Make one clear point per mark available.
    • Use correct technical terms rather than everyday language.
    • Do not repeat the question back to the examiner.

    Calculation questions

    • Write the formula first, then substitute the figures.
    • Show every step of your workings: method marks are often available.
    • Include units (£, %, units) and check your answer is sensible.

    Extended answers: PEEL

    • Point: make a clear, relevant statement.
    • Evidence or example: support it with an example, ideally from the scenario.
    • Explain: show why or how it matters, using "because" or "which means that".
    • Link: connect back to the question.

    For evaluate, assess and discuss questions, consider advantages and disadvantages for the business in the scenario, then finish with a justified conclusion that makes a judgement. Extended responses are often marked in levels, so balanced, applied reasoning with a clear conclusion is what moves an answer into the top level.

    Worked example. "Explain one benefit to Brightway Office Supplies of producing a cash flow forecast. (2 marks)"
    A strong answer: The forecast shows Brightway in advance which months it may run short of cash (1), which means it can arrange an overdraft or delay spending before it is unable to pay its suppliers (1).
